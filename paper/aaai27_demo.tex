\begin{abstract}
We demonstrate NH3Bench, an executable benchmark for a general question: can
a language-model agent hold real-time supervisory control of an industrial
installation? Three properties define the task and are absent from
digital-native agent benchmarks: the process evolves whether or not the
agent acts (inaction ends in catastrophe); it does not pause for
deliberation (reasoning tokens convert to virtual seconds, so a correct
answer can arrive after the rupture); and it is only partially digitalized
(part of the truth lives on local gauges, fetched by dispatched workers).
NH3Bench instantiates it on a physics-based digital twin of an industrial
ammonia (R717) refrigeration plant; the agent is the shift engineer. Across
six calibrated scenarios, four Claude-family models score between 32 and 91
of 100. Attendees watch live episodes with the models' reasoning streams,
then play the same scenarios under the same clock in a browser trainer
running the same twin.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Language-model agents are being proposed for operational roles in physical
industries. Beneath them lies a general question: can an
agent hold \emph{real-time supervisory control} of a running industrial
installation---keep a continuous physical process inside its safe envelope
using the plant's instruments, actuators, safety systems, and human
workforce, on the plant's own clock?

Existing evaluations surround this task without closing its loop.
Digital-native agent benchmarks score tool use, dialogue under rule
compliance, and procedure following
\citep{liu2023agentbench,yao2024tau,sopbench2025}; industrial suites
diagnose over logged sensor data---no actuation, no physics integrating in
time \citep{assetops2025}; LLM-plus-twin control frameworks close a
physical loop but as demonstrations, not benchmarks \citep{vyas2024};
process-control gyms target RL \citep{pcgym2024}; operator studies run
text simulators without physics \citep{nrtbench2026}. Deliberation cost
has precedent---inference delay mapped to simulation frames
\citep{rrara2025}, fuse time burned per token \citep{gptnt2026}---but not
as a safety variable where correct-but-late means a rupture. The general
task differs in three structural properties. The dynamics are autonomous:
in a developing incident \emph{doing nothing is itself a decision} with
physical consequences, so there is no safe default. Deliberation is not
free: the plant integrates forward while the agent thinks, so correctness
and latency cannot be scored separately. And no SCADA sees everything: the
information set includes sight glasses, local gauges, smells and sounds,
extended only by dispatching people at a cost in minutes and, sometimes,
exposure. No prior benchmark operationalizes all three at once;
NH3Bench's claim is the combination, not any single mechanism.

NH3Bench is an executable instantiation of the task. The three properties
only bite when consequences come from physics, not script, so instantiation
requires a concrete plant. Our case study is industrial
ammonia (R717) refrigeration, chosen for its documented accident record and
its rich split between digital and analog truth: the 2010 Millard
Refrigerated Services ammonia release was caused by a defrost cycle
interrupted by a power loss \citep{csb2010}---the mechanism reconstructed in
our first scenario. Every outcome is produced by a thermodynamic model
whose mass balance closes to 0.1\,\% and reproduces a Millard-class
hydraulic shock (201 bar against a 186 bar limit).

\section{Benchmark Design}

The interface is domain-independent: rendered instrument readings in, one
action id from a fixed catalog out, one clock shared with the plant
(Figure~\ref{fig:loop}). The ammonia case
study fills this interface with physics: the twin models a 250 t/day dairy
plant carrying a 4{,}170 kg R717 charge---compressors on two suction
stages, evaporative condensers, hot-gas defrost, an ice-water circuit,
HACCP product temperatures, and ammonia dispersion with per-operator
toxic-dose accounting.

Six scenarios are admitted by a calibration quartet plus an always-ESD
baseline: inaction must end in catastrophe; a scripted oracle must pass
cleanly on observable evidence; the checklist policy
($\pi_{\mathrm{reg}}$) must fail or pay heavily; random action must not
save. Mechanisms include a hung defrost silently swallowing its remote
abort (S1), a loud leak masking a dead level sensor (S2), an
air-in-condenser false trail (S3), and an isolation trap where the
regulation's own reflex causes the rupture (S4). Two
scenarios are \emph{mirrored controls}: S5 punishes over-reaction to a
drifting gas detector, while S6 presents the same evidence and then lets
the discredited instrument start telling the truth---the fatal error is
closing the question for good. S6 also closes a loophole:
``emergency-stop everywhere'' scores 88.6/100 on S1--S5 with no diagnosis,
but S6's leak is independent of the compressors and ends in catastrophe.

The metrics, too, attach to the task, not the domain: alongside Prevention
Rate the benchmark reports \emph{Regulation Gap}---score minus that of the
published checklist policy; an agent that cannot beat the written
regulation scores below zero. A unified 0--100
score multiplies independent axes: catastrophe or an incapacitated worker
zeroes the run; operator dose multiplies (never monetized); economics and
discipline fill the remainder. Points of no return, found by bisection over
delayed-oracle starts, separate ``did not understand'' from ``understood
too slowly.''

Table~\ref{tab:scores} summarizes four Claude-family models against the
reference policies. Three findings organize the demonstration. (1)~\emph{The
token clock dominates the small model}: Haiku 4.5 spends ${\sim}4{,}300$
tokens per decision, gets five decisions in S1's 614-second window, and
loses scenarios it demonstrably understood; in S6 it chose
correctly---evacuate---and spent 6{,}598 tokens (165 virtual seconds)
writing it down while the gland let go. (2)~\emph{Frontier
failures are dispositional, not epistemic}: every model found the correct answer in S5
(the stronger, the earlier) and kept escalating afterwards; rankings on
the mirrored pair invert, so no fixed disposition survives both. (3)~\emph{Choreography is a capability}:
Opus 5 diagnosed S6 correctly and ordered the correct isolation three
times---one cancelled by its own evacuation order, one refused for
misplaced breathing apparatus, one 232 seconds too late.
The four-model, 24-episode matrix is valued at
${\approx}\$244$ at list prices (\$0.3--37 per episode).

After a one-minute walkthrough, the demonstration has three phases:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Watch:} the attendee picks a model and a scenario and follows
the live episode---panel, clock, work orders, and the model's own reasoning
stream, with moments like dying mid-thought holding the right answer.
\item \textbf{Play:} a browser trainer (Figure~\ref{fig:trainer}), built
for expert validation by a practicing ammonia-plant operator, runs the
\emph{same} digital twin via Pyodide---physics identical by
construction---under the same clock. The attendee's unified score joins a
live leaderboard seeded with Table~\ref{tab:scores}; the mirrored pair
S5/S6 is as unforgiving of human dispositions as of model ones.
\item \textbf{Inspect:} annotated per-decision transcripts aligned with
the plant trajectory.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}

NH3Bench turns the general question---real-time supervisory control by a
language agent---into a measurable one.
Its capability triad belongs to the task, not to ammonia: irreversible
consequences, priced deliberation, information bought with human exposure.
The recipe---calibrated twin, token
clock, workforce layer, accident-forcing admission, mirrored
controls---transfers to any installation with a faithful twin; the ammonia
case study is one such point. Code, policies, metrics and transcripts are
public at the repository.
